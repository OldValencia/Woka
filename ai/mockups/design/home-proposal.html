Home, quieter — v2

The agreed design. Three things from the first proposal are gone: the “Linked folders” section, the caption under the card, and moving search up into the bar. The name and metadata are drawn on the cover instead, over a scrim that runs from the bottom edge to the middle. Folder size is answered by a tooltip on the path — hovering the folder you are standing in, not each of the twelve you can see. Nothing at all is left on the right of the bar: search stays in the dock where it was, and grid density lives in Ctrl+wheel, the background menu, and a readout in the middle of the frame. The toggles below compare all of this against what the app does today.

What was accepted

A · ACCEPTED

Calm colour at rest

The cover is drawn desaturated and slightly darkened, and comes to full strength under the cursor. The screen becomes readable immediately, and it costs one ColorFilter on the preview.

FolderCardPreview.kt · no model change, no grid change.
B · ACCEPTED

One cover instead of a mosaic

Removes 18 of the 24 competing pictures. “There is more inside” is said by the stacked edge under the tile, not by a collage.

FolderCoverMosaic.kt leaves the card; FolderCardPreview takes the first frame. Side effect: fewer decodes per tile.
C · ACCEPTED

A top bar that says something

Sixty pixels in the most prominent part of the window held three Windows buttons and nothing else. Home now puts its title and folder count on the left — and that is all it puts anywhere.

Nothing appears on the right. Search stays in the dock, beside the settings gear, where it already was; the bar gains nothing by taking it. Density gets no permanent control either — see the section below. Inside a folder the bar keeps the Back arrow and the path, and carries no screen counter: that went into the tooltip.

A new screen slot, not a new component: leadingAccessory, and only on Home. Density is stored in folderLayouts["HOME"]; both screens walk one ladder.
D · ACCEPTED, DIFFERENTLY

Name and count, on the cover

Neither a row under the tile nor a badge in the corner: the name sits bottom-left on the cover itself, with type and size beneath it. The scrim runs from the bottom edge to the middle, so the card lost 34 px of height and the cover did not end up orphaned.

FolderCardCaption.kt (scrim + two lines) · FolderCardBody.kt loses its bottom block.
E · ACCEPTED

Size, as a tooltip on the path

Hover a path segment: item count and total size for that folder. Not on the tile in the grid — the tile already wrote its count in the caption, and “how much is in here” is asked about the folder you are standing in, not about each of the twelve on screen. It is also what keeps the counter out of the bar, where it would hang permanently.

The name appears only if the path had to ellipsise it. If it fits, the cursor is already pointing at it and a second copy is noise. If it does not, the full name is the tooltip's first line. Truncation is decided by the text layout itself (hasVisualOverflow), not by counting characters: what fits is a question about glyphs.

New FolderInfoTooltip.kt, called from SmartBreadcrumbs.kt · the walk that measures size runs only while the tooltip is up, cached against mtime · pointer-events are off, so a click still navigates.
F · ACCEPTED

The path, flattened

The breadcrumbs used to be a shadowed, bordered pill floating in the middle of the bar: a container that looked like a control, did not behave like one, and drifted sideways whenever the path changed length. It is now plain text beside the Back arrow — so both screens have the same anatomy, identity on the left and nothing on the right.

SmartBreadcrumbs.kt loses its surface; GlobalTopBar.kt moves it out of the centre slot. Losing the container costs the affordance it provided, so a segment answers hover with its colour instead.
G · ACCEPTED

The dock and the bottom edge

Grid padding the height of the dock plus a gradient, so a photograph slides under the island instead of colliding with it. “+” moves into the dock as its first item — one island, not two.

HomeScreen.kt (padding) · FloatingDock/DockSecondaryItem (the button).
H · REJECTED

A “Linked folders” section

A section header with a rule is not needed — Home has no other group, and the band eats 40 px for one word. The grid starts directly under the bar.

What remains from it is the middle-truncation of long names — FolderCardUtils.kt.

How the caption is built

Two lines, both bottom-left, both inside the cover. The upper one is the name; the lower one is what used to be the word “Folder” and the “51 ITEMS” badge at the same time. The separator is a middle dot, which is why one line serves a folder and a file equally.

The same card inside a folder

The meta line is not tied to folders — it is the very same component. Inside a Linked Folder the first line is still the file's name and the second takes type, duration and size. The Back arrow stays, and so does the path — but the path is now plain text beside the arrow rather than a pill in the middle of the bar, which is what gives this screen the same left-anchored anatomy as Home. There is no screen counter in the bar: how many files a folder holds and what it weighs is answered by hovering a segment, not by a line that hangs there all the time. Hover both segments — the long one, which the bar had to ellipsise, opens with its full name; the short one goes straight to count and size.

Density without a control

The S / M / L segment is gone from the bar entirely. A permanent switch occupies the most prominent row of the window for a setting touched once a session, and all it can show is the current state. The readout in the middle costs nothing at rest and says what changed into what — the half a segmented control could never show. Two gestures move it: Ctrl+wheel (now on Home too, which was the only grid that answered to neither) and the background menu on right-click.

Home's menu is not the folder menu with items removed: “Create folder” and “Pin to Library” are questions about a directory you are standing in, and Home is not one. Their place is taken by the only thing Home can be asked to make — a link — running the very same picker the dock's “+” does. The ends of the ladder are shown disabled rather than hidden: a menu whose items move depending on state is one nobody builds muscle memory for. On the right the grid is already at L, which is why “Bigger” is greyed out.

Built in one sitting, outside the ticket flow, in stages with a build after each: the card, the tooltip on the path, the top bar, the dock, density.
